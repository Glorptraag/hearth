## Scenario

"Ivy (4.5) has been tracing the sandpaper letters for a few weeks. Today she was playing with the movable alphabet and I watched her lay out m-a-t and then look up at me and say 'mat!' She looked shocked. Then she did c-a-t. I didn't teach her to read, she just… did it."

## Interpretation

This is the explosion into reading that Montessori describes — reading emerging spontaneously from prior sensorial and language work (sandpaper letters for phonetic sound, movable alphabet for composition), not from direct reading instruction. The shock on Ivy's face is the discovery that the sounds she has been handling make words. It is the signature of reading arriving at the right moment in the sensitive period for language.

What to notice next: Ivy is in a wide-open window for written language. Make more movable alphabet work available, add phonetic reading cards, add simple phonetic books to the shelf. Do not formalise into "reading lessons" — the spontaneity is what makes the reading stick.
