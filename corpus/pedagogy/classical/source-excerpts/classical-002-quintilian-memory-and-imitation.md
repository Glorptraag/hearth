## Text

The chief symptom of ability in children is memory, of which the excellence is twofold, to receive with ease and retain with fidelity. The next symptom is imitation, for that is an indication of a teachable disposition, but with this provision, that it express merely what it is taught, and not a person's manner or walk, for instance, or whatever may be remarkable for deformity.

## Context

Watson's translation of Institutio Oratoria I.3.1–2 — the opening of the chapter on how a tutor reads the nature and capacity of a new pupil before deciding how to teach them. Verbatim claim, with two small residual doubts. Exact-phrase search returned the Honeycutt online edition of Watson's translation for "The next symptom is imitation … teachable disposition", and search summaries drawn from that page gave both sentences in this form, which also matches my recollection of the text. What I could not settle from this session: whether the 1856 print reads "of which the excellence is twofold" (as here) or "the excellence of which is twofold", and whether "provision" is followed by a comma (as here) or a colon — the Honeycutt edition appears to use a colon. Every word is as I believe Watson has it; Drew should check those two points against the registered scan before flipping suggestedDraft. Hearth's reading: this is the passage that makes memory work and imitation the tradition's first evidence of a learning mind — which is why a classical parent watches recitation and copywork so closely — while Quintilian's own caveat (imitate what is taught, not people's mannerisms) is the tradition's first restraint on it.
