## Text

In the practical course he gave the first Waldorf teachers in August 1919, Steiner asked that letters should grow out of artistic activity rather than be presented as a finished alphabet. The child first paints and draws. From a drawn picture — a fish, say, or the curve of a mouth — the teacher leads over, step by step, to the letter whose sound the picture carries, so that the letter arrives as something that came from the world and from the child's own hand rather than as an arbitrary sign to be memorised. For the same reason he wanted writing taught before reading: writing engages the whole child, hand and will as well as head, and reading can then be drawn out of what the child has already written. The order mattered to him because the abstract letter is a late invention of humankind; he wanted the child to walk something of the path from picture to sign, as people once did, rather than be handed the end product and asked to accept it.
