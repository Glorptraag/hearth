## What it indicates

The unschooling tradition treats the child's general state — emotional security, curiosity, willingness to try new things, quality of relationships, presence of joy — as the master evidence that the approach is working. This is not soft data in the unschooling frame; it is the most reliable signal available. A child who is academically "on track" but anxious, performance-focused, and afraid to be wrong is, in this tradition, showing an educational failure regardless of test scores. A child who is curious, secure, willing to try, and at home in their own life is showing educational success regardless of what they have or have not formally learned.

## Look for

- Child engages willingly with new situations and people
- Child is not afraid to attempt things they might fail at
- Child's emotional baseline is generally settled
- Child has trusted relationships with parents and others
- Child shows curiosity about the world rather than avoidance of it
- Child can be alone with themselves without distress
- Child's questions are open ("how does this work?") rather than performance-shaped ("am I right?")
