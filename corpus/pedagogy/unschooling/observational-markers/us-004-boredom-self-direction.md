## What it indicates

A child who, faced with an empty afternoon, can move through the boredom phase and arrive at their own choice of what to do is demonstrating something the unschooling tradition treats as developmentally significant. The capacity is not innate — it is built through repeated experience of being trusted to navigate boredom without rescue. Children who have always had their time structured by adults often lose this capacity and need to rebuild it during the deschooling phase.

## Look for

- Child can be told "I'm not going to suggest something — what do you want to do?" and eventually arrive at an answer
- The arriving sometimes takes a long time and may include complaint — both are normal
- Over weeks and months, the latency from boredom to self-direction shortens
- The child's self-chosen activity is genuinely their own, not a guess at what the parent wants
- The child does not require constant adult company to have a good day
