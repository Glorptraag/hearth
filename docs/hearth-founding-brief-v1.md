## 1. Purpose — Why Hearth Exists

Homeschooling is growing across the Western world, and for many families it is the right decision. But the decision to educate at home carries a weight that doesn't lift after the first week. Parents — overwhelmingly mothers — feel alienated from a system that was never designed for them. They feel unqualified to do what trained teachers do. They carry constant anxiety about whether they're doing enough, doing it right, and whether their children will be okay.

That anxiety has an institutional face. Every Australian family that home educates answers to a state regulator — Queensland's Home Education Unit, NSW's NESA, Victoria's VRQA, and their counterparts in every state and territory — each with its own registration process, reporting cycle, and evidentiary expectations. To the department, the paperwork is routine administration. To the parent, it is an annual examination of whether she is allowed to keep doing the most important work of her life, conducted in a professional vocabulary ("learning areas," "work samples," "educational programs") that does not describe how learning actually happens at her kitchen table. Whenever this document and its descendants say "compliance," that is the emotional weight the word carries — not form-filling, but the fear of being found insufficient by the institution that holds the permission slip. *(Added 2026-06-10: this paragraph generalises what was previously framed QLD/HEU-only; per-jurisdiction terminology lives in `src/config/jurisdictions.ts`.)*

Resources and communities help, but they don't address the heart of the problem. The heart issues — inadequacy, isolation, the gap between what a parent knows intuitively and what the education system formalises professionally — are hard to reach with a curriculum pack or a Facebook group.

Hearth exists to be the antidote to that.

Not by replacing formal education with something lighter. Not by telling parents their kitchen table already counts (though it often does). But by putting the real tools of education — pedagogy, learning frameworks, evaluation models, the professional knowledge that teachers spend years acquiring — into the hands of the people who need them most and have the least access to them.

The name is not branding. Learning used to happen around a hearth. The home was a centre of creation, learning, gathering, and growth long before the institution claimed those functions. Hearth is a callback to what the home already is — and an assertion that it can be again, with the right support.

## 2. Mission — What Hearth Does

**Hearth makes pedagogy and the professional frameworks of education accessible to families who educate at home.**

It bridges the gap between what parents already do and the academic insight, learning frameworks, and evaluation structures that formal education relies on. It provides the lens to see what's already happening clearly — and the tools to go further with confidence.

Hearth is a learning management system, but that description serves developers, not families. To a parent: Hearth is the thing that makes you a better educator, shows you what your children are really learning, handles the compliance paperwork, and gets out of your way.

### What this means in practice

Hearth serves families at every point on the confidence spectrum:

- **A parent with existing resources** who needs tracking, insight, and the AI-assisted pedagogical lens that turns "we read a book about frogs" into documented literacy, science, and comprehension evidence.
- **A parent who wants structured content** — packs, modules, and curated learning pathways designed by educators and shaped by the family's own pedagogical profile.
- **A parent who is growing in confidence** and beginning to design their own learning — building modules, recognising capability threads, and eventually formalising their own pedagogical thinking.

The platform handles all three. It does not privilege one over the others. A family that logs spontaneous learning and a family that follows a ten-week Classical pack are both using Hearth the way it was designed.

## 3. Vision — What the World Looks Like If Hearth Succeeds

### Near term (launch)

Queensland families using Hearth feel confident in their education, produce HEU-compliant documentation without stress, and discover capabilities in their children they hadn't formally recognised. The "am I doing enough?" anxiety is replaced by evidence.

### Medium term (2–3 years)

Hearth is the dominant homeschool platform in Australia. Expansion to the United States and Canada — markets with booming homeschool growth and significant regulatory variation. The platform handles jurisdiction-specific compliance generically: **Hearth makes home education legible to the institutions that regulate it**, regardless of whether that's Queensland HEU, a US state department of education, or a Canadian provincial authority.

### Long term (5+ years)

Hearth holds a controlling market position. Homeschool content creators and curriculum publishers come to the platform rather than competing with it. The product achieves robust LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability) standards compliance and industry recognition from major educational institutions.

The Constellation's capability data — DLOs, capability threads, portfolio evidence — speaks directly to what universities and secondary schools look for in admissions. Hearth enables families to hopscotch high school as a gate to tertiary education by producing evidence of genuine capability that institutions recognise and trust.

The Hearth brand carries trust, innovation, and — among long-tenure families — gratitude.

### The mature user

It's a mistake to frame Hearth as training wheels that families outgrow. A gifted carpenter might outgrow a ruler or a building code handbook. He doesn't outgrow a hammer and wood. Hearth grows with families: competent usage leads to advanced usage, advanced usage leads to new mastery.

The family that started by logging morning reading and kitchen measurement is now building custom modules, exploring the Constellation's cross-domain connections, contributing to community forums, and formalising pedagogical insights in a rich wiki. They don't need Hearth less — they need it differently, and more deeply.

Plan for that advanced user. Capture and meet their needs. The long-term product roadmap includes community forums, parent resources, and formalised pedagogy knowledge bases — not because retention demands it, but because these families earned it.

## 4. Values — What Hearth Refuses to Compromise

### 4.1 Christianity in the Public Square

Hearth is built by Christians, for Christian families, with a Christian worldview. This is not a "Christian version of an LMS." It is the best homeschooling platform there is, and it happens to be built by people who believe in a living God who made us and gives our lives meaning.

The analogy is the Christian cobbler. Christian workmanship is not slapping "Jesus" in Papyrus on every screen. It is a standard of excellence and intention that drives life and vocation. The platform is not missional. It is not a mission field. It does not dose users with verses or slip in reminders. It simply asserts that Christianity is true — because it is — and treats that assertion as unremarkably as a family that may have no interest in organic chemistry or quantum mechanics would treat those subjects.

Hearth rejects the paradigm that Christianity needs to contextualise itself, apologise for its presence, or ghettoize itself on its own platform. It was in the public square for centuries. We put it back there.

**What this means for content:** Classical education is Christian education. The idea that you could extricate classical education from the Christian framework and reconstitute it from a "secular" or Muslim foundation is an anachronism. If a family from another tradition feels tension with this, that is understandable and Hearth is not designed to force anything down throats — but the tension is with the Western tradition itself, not with Hearth's editorial choices. Empathetic FAQ and forum messaging can address this clearly and gently. The platform does not restructure its content to avoid the tension.

**What this means for pedagogy:** Pedagogy is philosophy-neutral. Charlotte Mason, Classical, Montessori, Waldorf/Steiner, Unschooling, Eclectic — these are teaching methods. The pedagogical overlay system interprets activities through the family's chosen lens. Christianity is not a pedagogical lens. It is the worldview in which all the lenses sit.

**What this means for families who aren't Christian:** There is plenty on the platform for them. Membership-included content, the logger, the Constellation, the compliance tools — none of these require faith commitment. Christian content in packs (Scripture memory, devotionals, catechism, Biblical chronology) is clearly present in Christian packs and clearly labelled. A non-Christian family is not being tricked or ambushed. But the platform does not mute itself to make them more comfortable.

### 4.2 Parent Empowerment, Not Platform Dependency

Hearth builds educational confidence. The goal is not an addicted user base — it is a mature, effective, confident homeschool community. The mark of a Hearth family should be competence, not chronic anxiety-driven platform usage.

This means:
- **Teach the framework, not just the content.** Packs include facilitator guidance that explains *why* an activity works, not just *how* to do it. Parents learn to recognise learning progressions, not just follow instructions.
- **Build tools that reward mastery.** The Module Builder exists because parents who've internalised the framework should be able to create their own learning experiences — and those experiences should be as effective as anything pre-authored.
- **Never weaponise anxiety for engagement.** Notifications are invitations, not obligations. The system assumes good faith. Sparse logging is not neglect — it may mean the family is using another method, travelling, or taking an intentional break.
- **Grow with families, don't cap them.** The product deepens as the user matures. Advanced analytics, community forums, pedagogy wiki, custom badge creation — these are the natural evolution for families who've been on the platform for years.

### 4.3 No Freemium Framing

Hearth refuses to make families feel like they're getting less than they deserve. Membership-included content carries zero transactional UI. There is no "upgrade to unlock" language in the daily experience. The Marketplace exists for additional content, but the core platform and the Starter Pack deliver full value from day one.

The deeper principle: families who chose homeschooling already carry enough anxiety about whether they've chosen wisely. The last thing they need is a platform that makes them feel like they can't afford the good version.

### 4.4 The 5-Minute Rule

Every parent-facing interaction must be completable in under five minutes. This is not a guideline. It is a constraint that governs all interaction design.

The reason is practical: parents are logging learning while supervising children. The phone is in one hand. The other hand is catching a toddler. If Hearth demands sustained attention, it fails the people it was built for.

### 4.5 Assume Good Faith

The platform assumes families are educating their children. Always.

Sparse logging does not equal neglect. The system never judges, never compares families, never creates league tables or activity metrics that could be read as performance grades.

When a parent expresses difficulty — "I don't know what I'm doing," "I need help building lessons" — then and only then does the system surface structured support.

**On the question of actual neglect:** Hearth will not become a tool that enables educational abandonment. This is a risk register item for the business, not a product feature. Backend analytics may flag patterns (accounts with zero activity over extended periods, repeated minimal-effort log entries). These trigger out-of-app processes — not in-app interventions or UI-level judgment.

### 4.6 Retrospective-First, Not Retrospective-Only

The core interaction model is retrospective logging. Parents capture learning after it happens, because that's how homeschool families actually live — spontaneously, responsively, following children's interests and daily rhythms.

But Hearth is not exclusively retrospective. The Weekly Planner, Module Builder, and structured content packs all serve families who want to plan ahead. The distinction is primacy: the Logger is the heartbeat of the daily experience. Planning tools serve parents who are ready for them. The platform never assumes that forward planning is the "correct" way to homeschool.

## 5. The Change — Before and After Hearth

### The relief moment

Before Hearth: A parent finishes a morning of reading aloud, baking, and a nature walk. The kids are playing. She sits down with tea and a knot in her stomach. *Did we do enough today? Would HEU think this counts? I should write something down but I don't know what to say. The compliance form wants "learning areas" and I just read a book about frogs.* She opens a Google Doc, stares at it, closes it.

After Hearth: Same morning. Same tea. She opens Hearth, logs three things in three minutes. Hearth tells her what learning areas were touched, what capability threads are growing, what her child demonstrated. She smiles. Closes the app. The knot is gone.

### Beyond relief

The relief moment is the hook. It's what gets families through the first week. But it's not where the real value lives.

The real progression is: **anxiety → relief → space to breathe → time to dream → dreams realised → joy.**

When the documentation burden shrinks and the compliance anxiety fades, something opens up. The parent has mental bandwidth again. She starts exploring the Module Builder. She drags a few activities into the Weekly Planner — not because she has to, but because she has ideas now. She designs a module around something her child is fascinated by. She watches the Constellation light up in a new domain. She realises she's not just keeping up — she's *good at this*.

That's the change. Not just "the knot is gone." The parent becomes the educator she didn't know she could be.

## 6. Who Hearth Is Not For

Hearth is built for families who are genuinely committed to educating their children at home. It is not for everyone, and naming the exclusions sharpens the identity.

**Hearth is not for:**

- Someone who wants total manual control over compliance documentation — writing and editing their own HEU submissions from scratch, with no platform interpretation or assistance.
- Someone who is not open to using any platform content and wants only a blank tracking spreadsheet.
- Someone with no drive to improve their practice or formalise their approach over time.
- Families who use "homeschooling" as a label for not educating their children at all. Hearth will not become a tool for neglect. The platform assumes good faith and will never accuse — but it is designed for families who are doing the work, and its entire value proposition assumes genuine educational engagement.

## 7. Voice and Relationship

Hearth speaks in a first-person, warm, supportive voice — the "gentle friend" tone described in the Brand Guide and Dashboard Design Decisions.

The platform does have a personified relationship with the parent. This is intentional. Parents who homeschool often lack professional peers. They don't have a staffroom. The warmth and support of Hearth's voice meets a real need — not by replacing human connection, but by providing consistent encouragement that the parent can access at any time.

The voice is about meeting parent needs, not completing a persona. Hearth does not first-person itself as "Her" or give itself a character identity. But it doesn't shy away from warmth, encouragement, and pedagogical insight delivered in a personal tone.

This empowers the parent-child relationship rather than clouding it. The parent who feels seen and supported by the platform is a more confident, more present educator. The alternative — a clinical tool that tries to "help parents be friends with their children" — would be presumptuous and less effective.
