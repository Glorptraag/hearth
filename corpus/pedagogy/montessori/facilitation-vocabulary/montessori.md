## Verbs

- **Prepare** — Set up the material, the space, the sequence, the cloth for spills. Most of Montessori facilitation happens before the child arrives.
- **Present — silent, slow, whole** — When the child is ready, demonstrate the activity once, slowly, silently, showing each movement clearly and separately. Language is minimal and structured (the three-period lesson). The presentation is a gift, not a lesson.
- **Withdraw** — Step back. Go do something else in the room. Resist eye contact, questions, prompts, encouragement. Withdrawal is not coldness — it is the creation of the space the child needs to make the material his own.
- **Observe from distance** — Take mental notes on what the child does. Do not photograph disruptively, do not comment, do not intervene unless there is a safety issue or material misuse.
- **Receive the completion** — When the child finishes and looks up, meet the look with a small smile. Not praise. Not a suggestion. Not a new lesson. The cycle is complete; honour it by doing nothing to extend it.

## Restraints

- Do not ask "What are you making?" mid-work.
- Do not say "Good job", "well done", or "so clever".
- Do not offer a different activity while the child is concentrating.
- Do not finish the task for the child to save time.
- Do not photograph with commentary.
- Do not speak more than necessary during presentation.
- Do not re-present the same material the same day the child first saw it.
- Do not name the activity's "goal" in outcome terms ("we're learning to count to ten today").
- The one-line rule: if a sentence would not change what the child does, do not say it.

## Micro-scripts

- **When the child finishes a work cycle and looks up** — (A small smile. Nothing said. The cycle is complete.)
- **When a material is being misused** — "The cubes are not for bashing. They go back on the shelf." (One short sentence, return the material, offer an alternative.)
- **When the child is deep in concentrated work** — (Nothing. Observation from distance. The concentration is the point.)
