## Text

A guideline that has circulated in unschooling communities for decades suggests that families leaving conventional school need approximately one month of decompression for each year the child was previously in school — a child leaving after grade five would need roughly five months of low-pressure recovery before unschooling can really begin. Practitioners describe this period in three rough phases: relief and celebration of the new freedom, then a period of boredom and complaint as institutional rhythms fall away, then the gradual emergence of authentic interest. The parent typically needs to deschool too, often for longer than the child.
