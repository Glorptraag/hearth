Notes
Activities
1
Re-read the profile together. Ask your child to close their eyes and picture their friend. What colours do they see? What's the first feature they notice — eyes, hair, a hat, wings?
2
Start with basic shapes. Draw a large oval for the head, a body shape below. Don't worry about detail yet — get the proportions and posture first. Is the friend tall? Bouncy? Shy and hunched?
3
Add personality features. Big curious eyes for an adventurous friend. Wild hair for an energetic one. A gentle smile for a kind one. Each design choice should connect to a character trait from the profile.
4
Choose and apply colours. Why those colours? Ask: "If your friend were a colour, what would they be?" Let the child experiment with pencils, watercolours, collage, or digital drawing.
5
Add the finishing details. Clothes, accessories, a background hint. Maybe they're holding something from their backstory. Maybe there's a detail only someone who read the profile would understand.
What to watch for
Deliberate design choices that reference character traits from the written profile — not just random decoration
Willingness to iterate ("That doesn't look right, let me try the eyes again") — shows self-assessment developing
Colour choices explained with reasoning ("Blue because she loves the ocean")
Attention to expressive details — body language, facial expression, gesture
Comparison to the written profile ("Wait, I said she had curly hair — I need to change this")
Pedagogical Lens
Charlotte Mason
This is picture study in reverse — instead of observing someone else's art, the child externalises their own mental image. Encourage careful, unhurried work. Quality of attention matters more than artistic skill.
Montessori
Prepare the environment with multiple art materials available. Let the child choose their medium. The act of translating abstract (personality) into concrete (drawing) develops classification and representation skills.
Waldorf
Begin with wet-on-wet watercolour to capture mood and colour before form. Let the character emerge from colour rather than starting with outlines. The friend is born from feeling first, shape second.
