## Reasoning

The no-limits position holds that screens contain genuine learning, that limits create hoarding, that restriction signals distrust, and that children given unrestricted access typically self-regulate over time. This is the dominant published position among the most prominent contemporary unschooling writers (Sandra Dodd, Joyce Fetteroll, Pam Laricchia, Sue Patterson).

Counter-position: a minority position within the movement, and a much larger position among adjacent self-directed learning families, holds that contemporary digital design contains attention-hijacking elements that did not exist in earlier generations of media, that some children show genuine compulsive patterns, and that complete non-intervention can cause harm.

Honest status: empirical evidence is genuinely lacking on both sides. The debate is the most heated within the movement. Hearth's framing should present both positions without resolving them.
