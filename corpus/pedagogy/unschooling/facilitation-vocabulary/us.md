## Verbs

- **Strew** — Place something interesting in the child's path with no instruction, no expectation, and no follow-up. The strewing is complete the moment the item is placed.
- **Follow the interest** — When a child shows engagement with something, support it without redirecting it. If the child is interested in trains, get more train books, find a train museum, watch train videos together. When the interest fades, let it fade.
- **Trust the child** — Operationally, this means not intervening when intervention is not asked for, not testing comprehension, not requiring demonstration of learning, and not assuming the child needs a structure they have not asked for.
- **Be a partner** — Engage as a co-explorer rather than as a teacher. Answer questions truthfully. Say "I don't know — let's find out" when you don't know. Share what you find interesting without requiring the child to find it interesting.
- **Connect** — When the child wants something the parent cannot provide directly — a topic the parent doesn't know, a skill the parent doesn't have, a community the parent isn't part of — the parent's job is to find the connection, not to become the expert.
- **Deschool yourself** — The most operational verb in the tradition. The parent's own internalised assumptions about what learning looks like are the primary obstacle to unschooling working, and dismantling those assumptions is ongoing work.

## Restraints

- Do not test what the child has learned. The testing changes what learning is.
- Do not offer rewards for engaging with anything. The reward changes the engagement.
- Do not impose limits as a generic policy. Make decisions case by case in dialogue with the child.
- Do not manufacture interest where none exists. If the child is bored by a thing, the thing is boring to them right now.
- Do not catch up on what schooled children "should know" by a certain age. The "should" is the school's, not the child's.
- Do not perform unschooling for relatives, social media, or yourself. Authenticity is the load-bearing element.

## Micro-scripts

- **When the child asks a question** — "Hmm, I don't know. Want to look it up together?"
- **When the child shows you something** — "Tell me about it." (Not "good job.")
- **When the child is bored** — "Yeah, sometimes there's nothing happening. I'm here if you want to do something together, otherwise I'll be in the kitchen."
- **When the child wants to do something you find tedious** — "Sure." (And then actually do it without sighing.)
- **When the child wants to stop something** — "OK." (Not "are you sure? You've put so much work into it.")
- **When a relative asks what the child is learning** — "All sorts of things. He's really into [authentic current interest] right now."
