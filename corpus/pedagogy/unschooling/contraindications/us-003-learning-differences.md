## Reasoning

The purist position holds that all children develop at different rates, that "learning disability" is often a label imposed by school's narrow timeline, and that many children diagnosed in school environments would have been fine if simply allowed to develop without comparison.

Counter-position: specialists, and many experienced unschooling parents whose children have actual diagnoses, hold that some children genuinely do not learn certain skills without targeted, structured intervention — that a child with dyslexia, dysgraphia, or dyscalculia may need explicit teaching that the unschooling approach does not provide on its own. The honest position is that pure non-intervention can leave a child without skills they needed and would have wanted.

Honest status: this is the contraindication most likely to cause real harm if applied dogmatically. Hearth should present it explicitly. A child who appears to be struggling in a way that goes beyond developmental variation deserves assessment and potentially targeted support, and that does not have to mean abandoning the unschooling approach in other areas.
