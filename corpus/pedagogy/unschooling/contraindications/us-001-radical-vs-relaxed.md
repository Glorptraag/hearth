## Reasoning

The radical position, articulated forcefully by writers including Sandra Dodd and Idzie Desmarais, holds that unschooling is a single coherent philosophy: trust the child across all of life, not just academics. To restrict in some areas while not restricting in others, in this view, undermines the foundation of trust that makes the whole approach work. The child experiences the inconsistency as conditional trust.

Counter-position: relaxed or moderate unschoolers — including many practitioners who would still use the term unschooling — accept some areas of parental decision-making (sleep, food, sometimes screens) while extending child autonomy in education and most daily choices. They argue that radical unschooling exceeds what is responsible in a contemporary household with safety, health, and family-rhythm considerations.

Honest status: this is an unresolved internal disagreement. Neither position has empirical support over the other. Hearth families calling themselves unschoolers should know the disagreement exists and locate themselves within it consciously rather than accidentally.
