The Hunt

5-8 minutes · Find and observe creatures

Step 1 of 3 · Explore & Search

Now it's time to explore. Walk slowly through your outdoor space, looking for any small creature — insects, spiders, worms, snails, anything that moves or hides.

🗣 What to Say

"We're going on a creature hunt. Walk as slowly as you can — the slower we go, the more we'll find. Look under leaves, near rocks, in corners. Tell me the moment you spot something!"

⏸ Let them lead. Follow their pace and direction. Resist pointing things out — let them discover.

Give them space. Count to 5 in your head before offering any guidance. Their natural exploration is the learning.

When they find something, this is the key moment. Don't rush to identify it. Let them observe first.

🗣 What to Say

"Oh! You found one! Let's look really carefully before we do anything else. What do you notice about it?"

Wait. Count to five in your head. Let them look and think.

🗣 Follow-up prompts (only if needed)

"What colour is it?... How many legs can you count?... Is it moving or staying still?... What do you think it's doing?"

Now use the magnifying glass (or phone camera) to look even closer. This often creates a "wow" moment.

🗣 What to Say

"Now let's use our special tool to see things we can't see with just our eyes. Look through here — what do you see now that you couldn't see before?"

Let them hold the magnifying glass themselves if possible. The act of controlling the view builds engagement.

👁 Watch For
  • • Are they actually slowing down, or rushing?
  • • Do they look under and around things?
  • • What catches their attention first?
🎤 Quick Capture
🎯 Philosophy Lens
Charlotte Mason

This is "masterly inactivity" — be present, interested, and quiet. Let nature teach.

👶 Age Adjustments
Stay close. Celebrate every tiny discovery. One creature = complete success.
Give more independence. Challenge: "Find three different kinds."
Add specificity: "Find creatures in three different micro-habitats."
🔄 If Not Landing
Too wiggly
"10 jumps first, then scientist eyes."
Not engaging
"Find just ONE creature."
Rushing
"Find something before I count to 20."
