## Text

Ivan Illich's Deschooling Society argued that compulsory schooling does not merely fail to educate — it actively teaches the lesson that learning must be done by experts, in institutions, on a schedule, with credentials. The deeper harm of school, in his view, is not bad teaching but the institutional confusion of education with schooling, such that adults eventually cannot imagine learning happening any other way. Illich proposed that genuine learning is conversational, voluntary, and woven through ordinary life — not delivered through curriculum.
