# Hearth Pedagogy Corpora — Licensing Needs

> **Status:** Action document. Identifies in-copyright primary sources that need to be licensed, permission-sought, or intentionally paraphrased-with-attribution for the pedagogy knowledge base build.
> **Architecture reference:** `hearth-pedagogy-knowledge-base-architecture-v1.md`
> **Companion:** `hearth-pedagogy-corpus-charlotte-mason-v1.md` (proof of concept, no licensing needed — Mason is fully public domain)

## 1. Summary

Hearth's six-pedagogy knowledge base can be built from a mixture of public domain primary sources and in-copyright contemporary sources. The public domain coverage is strongest for Charlotte Mason, Classical, Montessori (early works), and Waldorf/Steiner. It is weakest for Unschooling, where almost all of the movement's authoritative voices died after 1925 and their works remain in copyright.

This document is organised by pedagogy. For each, it names:

- What is already public domain and available for free use
- What is in copyright and needs licensing, permission, or paraphrase
- Whether the pedagogy can be built to full depth without licensing action
- Recommended next steps for Drew

The short answer: Charlotte Mason needs no action. Waldorf/Steiner and Montessori (early works) need no action for a credible v1 corpus, though later-career works would strengthen them. Classical can be built from ancient/medieval public domain foundations plus careful paraphrase of modern articulations, but licensing one or two modern texts would meaningfully improve it. Unschooling cannot be built to full depth without either licensing John Holt's works or relying entirely on contemporary web-based practitioners whose materials are more variable in authority.

## 2. Charlotte Mason — No Action Needed

Mason died in 1923. Her complete six-volume Home Education Series (1886–1925) is public domain worldwide under the life+70 rule, and her American editions are public domain in the US under the pre-1929 rule. All six volumes are available in full text on Project Gutenberg and the Internet Archive.

**Public domain sources available:**

- *Home Education* (Vol. 1, 1886; Project Gutenberg #71087)
- *Parents and Children* (Vol. 2, 1896)
- *School Education* (Vol. 3, 1904)
- *Ourselves, Our Souls and Bodies* (Vol. 4, 1904)
- *Some Studies in the Formation of Character* (Vol. 5, 1905)
- *A Philosophy of Education* (Vol. 6, 1923 — Mason's final synthesis)

Parents' Review articles from the Parents' National Educational Union (PNEU) magazine Mason edited and contributed to are also public domain and add substantial additional primary material.

**In-copyright contemporary reinterpretations** (not needed for the corpus, but worth noting): Susan Schaeffer Macaulay's *For the Children's Sake* (1984, Crossway), Karen Glass's *Consider This* and *Know and Tell* (contemporary, Living Book Press), Sonya Shafer's Simply Charlotte Mason materials. None of these are needed — Mason's own voice is what the corpus calls for.

**Recommended action:** None. The Charlotte Mason Wave 1 proof of concept is already delivered. Proceed to full Wave 1 authoring.

## 3. Classical — Largely Obtainable, One Licensing Decision Recommended

Classical education is in an unusual position: its foundational texts are ancient and medieval, so the deepest sources are unambiguously public domain. The modern articulation of classical education as a homeschool method dates to Dorothy Sayers' 1947 essay and is continued by contemporary authors whose works are in copyright.

### 3.1 Public Domain Foundations (Free to Use)

The classical tradition rests on texts that are public domain worldwide under any reasonable rule:

- **Plato** — especially *Republic* Books II, III, and VII on education; *Laws* Book VII; *Meno* on learning and recollection. All Benjamin Jowett translations are public domain.
- **Aristotle** — *Nicomachean Ethics* Book II (on habituation and virtue), *Politics* Book VIII (on education). W. D. Ross translations are public domain.
- **Quintilian** — *Institutio Oratoria* (c. 95 CE). Books I and II are the foundational ancient text on the education of the young. H. E. Butler's Loeb translation is public domain for pre-1929 volumes.
- **Augustine** — *Confessions* Book I (on Augustine's own childhood education), *On Christian Doctrine* on the role of liberal arts.
- **Aquinas** — *Summa Theologiae*, especially questions on the teacher (Q. 117) and on habit (I-II, QQ. 49–54).
- **Erasmus** — *On the Method of Study* (*De Ratione Studii*, 1511), *On the Liberal Education of Children* (*De Pueris Instituendis*, 1529).
- **Comenius** — *The Great Didactic* (1649), *Orbis Pictus* (1658). M. W. Keatinge's translations are public domain.
- **John Milton** — *Of Education* (1644).
- **John Locke** — *Some Thoughts Concerning Education* (1693).
- **Cardinal Newman** — *The Idea of a University* (1852).
- **Charlotte Mason herself** — yes, Mason is also part of the Classical corpus. Modern classical homeschool writers like Karen Glass have argued that Mason is a classical educator (see *Consider This*, 2014). Her public domain works count toward the Classical corpus too.

These give the Classical corpus a very rich foundation. A full Classical knowledge base could arguably be built entirely from these texts.

### 3.2 In-Copyright Modern Articulations

The modern classical homeschool movement is articulated primarily in the following works, all still in copyright:

| Work | Author | Year | Rights Holder | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "The Lost Tools of Learning" (essay) | Dorothy L. Sayers | 1947 | Sayers estate (c/o David Higham Associates, UK) | **The single most-cited modern classical education text.** Still in copyright — Sayers died 1957, UK life+70 means 2027; US life+95 for corporate-held, but Sayers is individual author. The essay is widely quoted online but that does not make it PD. |
| *The Well-Trained Mind* | Susan Wise Bauer | 1999 (rev. 2016) | W. W. Norton | The single best-known contemporary classical homeschool guide. In copyright. |
| *The Core* | Leigh Bortins | 2010 | Classical Conversations Books | Foundational for the Classical Conversations movement. |
| *Recovering the Lost Tools of Learning* | Douglas Wilson | 1991 | Crossway | Christian classical education foundation. |
| *Beauty in the Word*, *Beauty for Truth's Sake* | Stratford Caldecott | 2012, 2009 | Angelico Press | Catholic classical. |
| Circe Institute articles | Andrew Kern and others | ongoing | Circe Institute | Contemporary classical pedagogy, largely blog-published. |
| *Norms and Nobility* | David Hicks | 1981 | Praeger | Out of print but in copyright. |
| *Climbing Parnassus* | Tracy Lee Simmons | 2002 | ISI Books | Defence of classical humanism. |

### 3.3 Recommendation for Classical

Build the v1 Classical corpus primarily from public domain foundations. These are substantial — Plato, Aristotle, Quintilian, Augustine, Comenius, and Mason alone provide more than enough material for a rich 50-excerpt Source Excerpts layer.

For the modern articulation, Drew has three options:

**Option A — No licensing, paraphrase with attribution.** Write Practice Patterns, Contraindications, and Facilitation Vocabulary that summarise the modern classical position in Drew's own words with clear attribution ("The modern classical movement, following Dorothy Sayers's 1947 essay 'The Lost Tools of Learning', argues..."). This is legally safe under fair use summary and standard academic practice. It loses the authority of direct quotation.

**Option B — License Sayers's "Lost Tools" essay.** This is a single short essay (~6,000 words) and is the load-bearing modern classical text. Licensing rights are held by David Higham Associates (UK). A one-time license for limited quotation in a commercial product is plausibly obtainable for a few hundred to low thousands of pounds. This would be the highest-leverage single licensing action for Classical.

**Option C — License a contemporary guide.** *The Well-Trained Mind* is the modern canonical homeschool guide. Licensing quotation from Bauer through W. W. Norton would be a substantial undertaking and likely expensive.

**Drew's recommended next step:** Proceed with Option A for v1. Revisit Option B (Sayers licensing) if Classical becomes a high-engagement pedagogy in alpha data and the corpus needs the direct voice of the modern movement's founding statement.

## 4. Montessori — Obtainable with Pre-1929 Works

Maria Montessori died in 1952. Under the life+70 rule her works entered public domain worldwide in 2023. However, US copyright law applies the 1929 cutoff (works first published before 1929 are US public domain), which gives Montessori a specific structure worth understanding.

### 4.1 Public Domain Works (US and Most Jurisdictions)

Pre-1929 works are public domain in the US and, under life+70, are public domain worldwide since 2023:

- **The Montessori Method** (1912, English translation by Anne George, Frederick A. Stokes Co.) — the foundational text. Contains Montessori's account of the Casa dei Bambini, prepared environment, sensitive periods, and the didactic materials. This is the single most important text for the corpus.
- **Pedagogical Anthropology** (1913, English translation by F. T. Cooper, Frederick A. Stokes Co.) — the scientific foundation of the method.
- **Dr. Montessori's Own Handbook** (1914, Frederick A. Stokes Co.) — Montessori's practical guide to the materials.
- **Spontaneous Activity in Education** / **The Advanced Montessori Method, Vol. 1** (1917, English translation by Florence Simmonds, Frederick A. Stokes Co.) — Montessori's development of the method for older children.
- **The Advanced Montessori Method, Vol. 2** / **The Montessori Elementary Material** (1917, English translation by Arthur Livingston, Frederick A. Stokes Co.).

All of these are available on Project Gutenberg, Internet Archive, and in several pre-1929 editions whose US public domain status is clear.

**This is a substantial public domain corpus.** It contains the concepts of the prepared environment, sensitive periods, the work cycle, self-correction in the materials, the role of the directress, practical life, sensorial education, and Montessori's view of the child's spontaneous development. A full v1 Montessori corpus could be built entirely from these sources.

### 4.2 In-Copyright Works (Post-1929 in the US; PD Worldwide Since 2023 Under Life+70)

The following are Montessori's later works. Their US copyright status is complicated; their worldwide status under life+70 is that they became PD in 2023. However, modern English translations and editions are held under separate copyright by the publisher.

| Work | Year | Publisher | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *The Secret of Childhood* (*Il Segreto dell'Infanzia*) | 1936 Italian; English 1936/1966 | Montessori Pierson Publishing / Ballantine | Italian text PD worldwide 2023. Modern English translation (Carter, 1966) still in copyright through the translator. |
| *The Absorbent Mind* (*La Mente del Bambino*) | 1949 Italian; English 1967 | Montessori Pierson Publishing / Holt | Italian text PD worldwide 2023. Claude Claremont's 1967 English translation still in copyright. |
| *From Childhood to Adolescence* | 1948 | Montessori Pierson Publishing | Italian PD worldwide 2023. |
| *To Educate the Human Potential* | 1948 | Montessori Pierson Publishing | Same. |
| *What You Should Know About Your Child* | 1948 | Montessori Pierson Publishing | Same. |
| *Education and Peace* | 1949 | Montessori Pierson Publishing | Same. |

The Italian originals of the late works are public domain worldwide since 2023. The English translations are separately copyrighted, with most of the canonical translations held by Montessori Pierson Publishing in Amsterdam (the official Montessori rights body).

### 4.3 Contemporary Montessori Practitioners

Modern Montessori writing (Paula Polk Lillard, Simone Davies, Junnifa Uzodike, E. M. Standing's biography) is in copyright and would need licensing if directly quoted.

### 4.4 Recommendation for Montessori

Build the v1 Montessori corpus entirely from the pre-1929 English translations, which are unambiguously public domain in the US and available on Project Gutenberg. This gives a rich corpus covering all the core Montessori concepts.

For the later works (*The Absorbent Mind*, *The Secret of Childhood*), the pragmatic path is:

**Option A — Use the Italian originals.** The Italian originals are PD worldwide since 2023. If Drew or a Montessori consultant can translate selected short passages directly from the Italian, these translations are Drew's own work and can be quoted freely. This requires Italian competency but is legally clean.

**Option B — Contact Montessori Pierson Publishing.** The Amsterdam-based official Montessori rights body. For a limited commercial license to quote a modest number of short passages from the canonical English translations, they have a history of being reasonable with educational users but rates vary. This is the right contact point for any licensing need.

**Option C — Paraphrase with attribution.** Drew's own summaries of the key concepts from *The Absorbent Mind* and *The Secret of Childhood*, with clear attribution and without direct quotation.

**Drew's recommended next step:** For v1, proceed entirely with pre-1929 PD works. The corpus will be substantial and authentic. If post-1929 Montessori concepts are needed (notably the fully developed absorbent mind framework), add Option C initially; consider Option B if Montessori becomes a high-engagement pedagogy.

## 5. Waldorf/Steiner — Obtainable, Deeper Than Expected

Rudolf Steiner died in 1925. His works are public domain worldwide under life+70 since 1996. For US copyright, most of his lectures were either published before 1929 or were lecture transcripts whose US publication happened later but with contested copyright status. The practical situation: Steiner's pre-1929 published lectures are unambiguously PD in the US, and his full corpus is PD worldwide.

### 5.1 Public Domain Foundations

The following Steiner works are unambiguously public domain:

- **The Education of Children from the Standpoint of Theosophy** (1907 lecture; 1911 English translation published by Rajput Press, Chicago) — the foundational Waldorf education text. Available in full on Project Gutenberg (eBook #55586). This is the single most important pre-Waldorf-school statement of Steiner's educational philosophy. Contains the seven-year developmental stages, imitation/example before age 7, authority/hero-worship ages 7–14, the role of the etheric body, and the critique of abstract intellectual education.
- **The Foundations of Human Experience** (1919 lectures to the first Waldorf teachers; also published as *Study of Man*) — the founding lectures of the Waldorf school movement.
- **Practical Advice to Teachers** (1919 lectures).
- **Discussions with Teachers** (1919 lectures).
- **The Kingdom of Childhood** (1924 lectures, Torquay) — late Steiner on child development.
- **Rudolf Steiner Archive** (rsarchive.org) — maintains a substantial public domain collection of Steiner's lectures with varying translation rights.

### 5.2 Translation Rights — A Wrinkle

The wrinkle in Steiner is that while the original German is fully PD, modern English translations by named translators (especially those done for SteinerBooks/Anthroposophic Press) may be in copyright under the translator's rights. The 1911 Rajput Press translation of *Education of Children from the Standpoint of Theosophy* is clearly PD (pre-1929 US publication). Later translations by named contemporary translators are held by SteinerBooks or the Rudolf Steiner Verlag in Dornach, Switzerland.

For v1 corpus building, rely on the 1911 Rajput Press translation and any other pre-1929 English translations. This gives a substantial foundation without licensing complications.

### 5.3 Modern Waldorf Practitioners

Contemporary Waldorf educators (Eugene Schwartz, Jack Petrash, Rahima Baldwin Dancy, Sharifa Oppenheimer) are in copyright. Their works are valuable for the "modern articulation" of Waldorf practice but are not needed for a coherent v1 corpus that speaks in Steiner's own voice.

### 5.4 Recommendation for Waldorf

Build the v1 Waldorf corpus from the 1911 Rajput Press translation of *Education of Children from the Standpoint of Theosophy*, supplemented by the 1919 founding lectures where pre-1929 English translations exist. This is enough source material for a full 50-excerpt Source Excerpts layer.

**Drew's recommended next step:** None for v1. Proceed with the public domain corpus. If a contemporary Waldorf practitioner's voice is needed later, consider Option C (paraphrase with attribution) rather than licensing.

**Note:** Waldorf/Steiner is the pedagogy that contains the most unusual metaphysical framing (etheric body, astral body, Theosophy/Anthroposophy). The corpus should treat this framing with honesty — it is part of the tradition as Steiner articulated it — while making clear to the parent reader that the practical pedagogy of rhythm, imitation, hands/heart/head, and delayed academic intellectualism does not require the metaphysical framework to be adopted. This is an authoring judgement rather than a licensing question, but it's worth flagging here because it differs from the other pedagogies.

## 6. Unschooling — The Hard Case

Unschooling has the most constrained licensing picture of any Hearth pedagogy. The movement's canonical voices all died after 1925, and their works remain in copyright. Unlike Classical (which can fall back on ancient sources) or Montessori (which has pre-1929 works), Unschooling has essentially no public domain primary corpus.

### 6.2 Contemporary Web-Based Practitioners

Several current unschooling practitioners publish primarily on the web and have varying terms:

- **Sandra Dodd** — maintains sandradodd.com with extensive writing on unschooling. Her stated site terms permit quoting with attribution for personal use; commercial quoting in a product like Hearth would need direct permission. Contact via her website.
- **Pam Sorooshian** — publishes on her own site and on Always Learning. Similar informal permissions situation.
- **Joyce Fetteroll** — publishes at joyfullyrejoycing.com. Similar.
- **Pat Farenga** — continues HoltGWS work and publishes at johnholtgws.com. Official conduit for John Holt's material and rights.

Contemporary unschooling practitioners generally write in plain blog form and are often approachable directly. A permission conversation with Sandra Dodd or Pat Farenga, framed as an educational platform that wants to honour the tradition authentically, is likely to be received favourably. It is the right first move.

### 6.3 Recommendation for Unschooling

This is the pedagogy that most needs a licensing conversation. There are three workable paths:

**Option A — Holt Associates permission for John Holt excerpts.** Pat Farenga runs HoltGWS (johnholtgws.com) and is the primary contact for John Holt's rights. A direct approach explaining Hearth's purpose, the scope of intended quotation (short attributed excerpts for a retrieval system, not reproduction of full works), and the commercial-but-mission-driven nature of the product is the correct first step. Historically, Holt Associates has been favourable to educational uses of Holt's material. A modest licensing fee is likely but not prohibitive.

**Option B — Sandra Dodd and contemporary practitioners.** A permission conversation with 2–3 contemporary unschooling writers, framed as wanting to represent their tradition authentically in a homeschool platform, is likely to succeed. This gives the corpus live modern voices that Holt alone would not provide, and grounds Unschooling in its actual contemporary practice rather than only its 1960s founding statements.

**Option C — Paraphrase with attribution, no direct quotation.** Legally clean but weaker. The corpus would read as "the unschooling tradition holds that..." rather than as John Holt's own voice. Acceptable for a launch but noticeably thinner than the other pedagogies.

**Drew's recommended next step:** Do all three in sequence. Start with Option B (contemporary practitioners) because those conversations are quickest and lowest-stakes. Move to Option A (Holt Associates) in parallel — this is a one-email opener and a response time of weeks is normal. Hold Option C in reserve as the v1 fallback if A and B don't resolve before the Unschooling wave needs to ship.

**Alternative framing:** Unschooling may be the pedagogy for which a "guest author" model works best. Rather than Hearth authoring the corpus against Holt's texts, Hearth commissions a named contemporary unschooling practitioner (with their permission and at fee) to author the Practice Patterns, Worked Examples, and Facilitation Vocabulary for the Unschooling knowledge base directly. The Source Excerpts layer is then populated with licensed Holt quotations. This gives the corpus the authority of a practising unschooler's voice rather than Drew's interpretation of the tradition. It is more expensive than DIY authoring but is the single highest-quality option if budget permits.

## 7. Eclectic — No Primary Corpus Required

Eclectic is a meta-lens rather than a tradition. It has no primary texts of its own. Its corpus is a retrieval strategy implemented in the write-time enrichment service: when an Eclectic family logs an entry, the retrieval pulls top chunks from two or three of the other pedagogies' corpora and presents the interpretations in parallel with explicit cross-tradition framing.

**No licensing needed.** The Eclectic lens inherits whatever licensing status the other corpora have.

## 9. Legal and Editorial Notes

**Fair use / fair dealing for a retrieval system.** There is a colourable fair use argument that short attributed excerpts used as retrieval context for an AI system, not reproduced directly to end users, fall under transformative fair use in the US and fair dealing in the UK/Australia. However, Hearth is a commercial product and Drew is not a lawyer. The safer position is to treat every quotation as requiring either public domain status, a license, or paraphrase. This document recommends that safer position throughout.

**Paraphrase with attribution is not copyright-clean by default.** A close paraphrase that preserves the structure and ideas of the original can still infringe, particularly if extended. True paraphrase means expressing the idea in substantially different language and structure, and always naming the source. When in doubt, write less and attribute more.

**Public domain translations.** Translations have their own copyright, separate from the original. A modern English translation of a 1907 German Steiner lecture is in copyright even though the German is public domain. The 1911 Rajput Press translation is PD because it's pre-1929 US publication. Always verify translation copyright separately from original copyright.

**Attribution standard.** Every corpus entry — even paraphrased — must name the author, work, and year. This is an editorial standard, not only a legal one. The pedagogies deserve this respect, and so do the parents Hearth is serving.
