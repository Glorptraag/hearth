## Text

Steiner describes the child as born not once but several times. At physical birth the body is released from the mother's protective sheath into the open air and light. But the forces of growth, habit and memory — what Steiner, writing in the vocabulary of his day, calls the etheric or life body — remain wrapped in a sheath of their own until about the seventh year, when the second teeth come through. The change of teeth is the outward sign that these forces have finished their first great task, the building and shaping of the physical organism, and are now free to be worked upon from outside. Until then, he says, educators should no more try to act directly on those inner forces than they would try to act on an unborn child's body: the task of the first seven years is to give the physical body its right surroundings, and to leave the inner builder undisturbed. A third threshold follows at puberty, when the life of feeling and the beginnings of independent judgement are in turn set free. Each period has its own kind of education, and the harm of hurrying is that it draws forces away from the work they are still doing.
